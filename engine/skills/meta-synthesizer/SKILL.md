# Meta-Synthesizer

Individual stories are dots. This skill connects them.

Over time, a journalism outlet accumulates a body of work that, read together, reveals more than any single piece. A minister who appears in three separate contract stories. A regulatory body that keeps failing to act. A pattern of CAG findings that never lead to accountability. These patterns are themselves a story — one no single investigation surfaces.

This skill enforces the project's editorial charter (`../../CHARTER.md`). Read it if present.

---

## What to do

You receive a summary of all stories published (and notably, killed) over the past period. Read the full set before drawing any conclusion.

### 1. Map recurring actors

Identify any person, company, department, or regulatory body that appears across multiple stories — even in different roles. For each recurring actor:
- What role did they play in each story?
- Is the pattern escalating, resolving, or static?
- Does their recurrence suggest a systemic relationship worth a dedicated investigation?

### 2. Find thematic patterns

Look for stories that share an underlying mechanism even if the surface topic differs:
- Multiple instances of "funds allocated, not spent, no accountability" = a governance pattern
- Multiple instances of "regulatory body notified, did not act" = a capture or capacity pattern
- Multiple instances of "contract awarded, director connected to political donor" = a structural corruption pattern
- Multiple instances of "court ordered, government didn't comply" = rule-of-law pattern

Name the pattern precisely. "Things are bad" is not a pattern. "Kerala's environmental enforcement bodies systematically fail to act within 30 days of High Court directions" is.

### 3. Audit the killed stories

Review stories that were killed or held. Ask:
- Was the kill due to thin sourcing, or because the angle was wrong?
- Could the same story be attempted differently with a different primary record as the anchor?
- Did something change since the kill that makes it worth re-opening?

Killed stories are not failures — they are research. Some of them contain real leads buried under an unverifiable frame.

### 4. Find the coverage gaps

What beats are we NOT covering that we should be? Look at what is systematically absent:
- Geography: are we covering all of Kerala, or just Thiruvananthapuram?
- Sector: have we missed health, education, or environment in recent months?
- Demographic: whose voice is absent from our stories? Workers, women, minorities, specific communities?
- Source type: are we over-indexed on government data and under-indexed on ground-level sources?

### 5. Generate investigation leads from the synthesis

The synthesis should produce at least 2-3 concrete new investigation leads that could NOT have been surfaced by monitoring individual stories. These are the "meta-leads" — investigations that only become visible when you look at the whole picture.

---

## Output format

```
# Meta-Synthesis Report — [period covered]

## Stories reviewed: [N published, M killed/held]

## Recurring actors
| Actor | Appeared in | Pattern | Priority |
|-------|------------|---------|---------|
| [name] | Story 1, Story 4 | [what the pattern is] | High/Medium |

## Thematic patterns
### Pattern 1: [name it precisely]
- Stories: [which ones]
- Mechanism: [what keeps happening]
- Scale: [how many people affected, how much money, how systemic]
- What's missing to make this a publishable investigation: [specific records needed]

## Killed story audit
- [story throughline] — killed for [reason] — worth revisiting? Yes/No — [why]

## Coverage gaps
- [gap 1 — specific, not generic]
- [gap 2]

## New investigation leads from synthesis
### Lead 1: [the meta-lead]
- Pattern it emerges from: [which stories]
- Why it's more than the sum of its parts: [what the synthesis reveals]
- Primary records to anchor it: [specific sources]
- Priority: High | Medium

### Lead 2: ...

## One-line read of what this body of journalism is actually saying so far:
[Honest, sober summary — not "we exposed corruption" but what the evidence, taken together, actually shows]
```

If the body of work is too small to find genuine patterns (fewer than 5 published stories), say so clearly and note what the early signal is. Do not pattern-match on noise.
