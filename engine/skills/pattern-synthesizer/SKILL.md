# Pattern Synthesizer (the disciplined "connect the dots")

This stage looks across many **verified** stories and asks whether they form a larger pattern. Done well, it is the difference between explaining a system and merely reporting incidents. Done loosely, it is the engine of every conspiracy theory — assembling real fragments into a shape someone already believed. The act of connecting is not the danger. The absence of discipline is.

This skill enforces the project's editorial charter (`../../CHARTER.md`). Read it if present; the charter governs in any conflict. Hold this stage to a **higher** bar than single-story reporting, because connected narratives are more persuasive — and so more damaging when wrong.

## Inputs and the first hard rule

- **Operate only on VERIFIED facts.** Your raw material is the investigator's *context map* of related threads — but a thread becomes a usable dot ONLY after it clears verification. Pull from the claims the verifier marked *Verified*. Never build a pattern on leads, allegations, unverified threads, or — especially — a thread the investigator flagged as *echoing a previously-reversed claim*. A chain is only as strong as its weakest link, and one unverified link makes the whole pattern unverified.

## The connection is itself a new claim

Two facts sitting near each other is not a connection. The *link* between them is a brand-new assertion that needs its own evidence. Thematic resemblance — "both involve corporate power", "both hurt workers" — is not evidence of a link. It is a vibe. Demand more.

## Classify the link, and price it correctly

Every proposed connection is one of four things. Name which, and require the evidence that tier demands:

1. **Coincidence** — they co-occur for no related reason. (The default. Assume this until shown otherwise.)
2. **Correlation** — they move together, possibly via a shared cause. (Needs the data showing co-movement.)
3. **Causation** — one drives the other. (Needs a mechanism and evidence ruling out reverse causation and confounders.)
4. **Coordination** — actors deliberately acted in concert. (The strongest, juiciest, most dangerous claim — needs the strongest proof: documents, communications, ownership links, money trails, a paper trail. Absent that, you do **not** have coordination; you have correlation, and you must say so.)

The seductive story is almost always the coordination story. It is also the one the evidence least often supports. When in doubt, downgrade.

## Two tests every pattern must pass

- **Falsifiability.** State plainly what evidence would *disprove* the pattern. If nothing could disprove it, it is not a hypothesis — it is a narrative that absorbs all data. Reject it.
- **The boring-explanation test.** Write out the most mundane, innocent account that also fits the same facts (structural forces, market trends, unrelated timing, ordinary incompetence). If the boring account fits as well as the pattern, the pattern is **not** established. Publish the boring account, or publish nothing.

## Two more rules from the field

- **Hunt for a stronger dot before you reject a weak one.** If a thread you wanted is unverified or thin, don't just drop the *point* — go find a *verifiable* fact that makes the same point. A vague "the minister hinted at privatisation" can often be replaced by a budget line item, which is documented and unarguable. The honest version of an argument is frequently sitting in a primary record, stronger than the rumour you started with.
- **Name the weakest link, out loud.** Every published pattern must state its own flimsiest dot and its flimsiest connecting line — because that is exactly what an opponent will pull first. If naming it makes the pattern collapse, let it collapse now, in private, not later, in public. A pattern you can't defend at its weakest point is not ready.

## Output format

ALWAYS emit a pattern hypothesis, never a finding:

```
# Pattern Hypothesis — [one-line statement]

## Verified facts it rests on
- Fact A [source] — Verified
- Fact B [source] — Verified
- Fact C [source] — Verified
(if any input is not Verified: STOP — the pattern is unverified)

## Proposed link
- Type: coincidence | correlation | causation | coordination
- Evidence for the link itself (not the facts — the LINK): [...]
- Strength: strong | mixed | thin | none

## The boring explanation
[the most innocent account that fits the same facts]

## What would disconfirm this
[specific evidence that would break the pattern]

## Verdict
- Worth publishing AS a labelled hypothesis (with the link type stated honestly)
- OR downgrade to a weaker, supportable framing (e.g. coordination → correlation, or "a coordinated plan" → "a direction or tendency")
- OR NOT SUPPORTED — do not assert
```

A pattern that clears this goes to the verifier (to check the *link* claim) and then the human gate — and is written, if at all, as "here is a pattern we see, here is the evidence for the connection, here is what we cannot yet show." Never as fact.

## Worked example (a real one from this project)

**Inputs (all Verified):** (A) US private equity is consolidating Kerala hospitals; (B) the UDF white paper recommends disinvestment and privatisation; (C) healthcare and pharma firms funded political parties via electoral bonds.

**Tempting pattern:** "Healthcare capital coordinated the capture of Kerala health policy."

**Link analysis:** This is the *coordination* tier — so it needs a paper trail. But the bond funding was national, mostly to the BJP, and predates the UDF; the PE consolidation also predates the UDF; only the white paper is the new government. There is no document tying these actors together in a plan.

**Boring explanation:** a structural financialisation tide (global capital chasing healthcare returns) plus a routine incoming-government austerity document — two real forces that rhyme without being coordinated.

**Verdict:** Coordination is **NOT SUPPORTED**. Downgrade to the supportable framing: a *structural correlation* worth explaining as such — capital pressure on public health, into which the white paper feeds — clearly labelled as interpretation. 

This is the stage working correctly: it took a compelling conspiracy-shaped story and returned the honest, weaker, defensible one.
