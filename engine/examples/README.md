# Examples — the engine, before and after

These two files are the same topic handled two ways. They're the clearest proof
of what the engine is *for*.

- **`masala-bonds-original-op-ed-BEFORE.md`** — the original opinion piece, written
  straight from a partisan brief ("the Congress government is trashing the
  communists / serving the rich"). Persuasive, and demolishable: it leans on the
  framing, blurs fact and interpretation, and omits the inconvenient evidence.

- **`masala-bonds-ENGINE-output.md`** — the same subject run through the full
  pipeline: topic-intake → investigate (+ context map) → verify (trust gate) →
  pattern-check → write (transparent perspective) → review. The partisan framing
  was treated as a *lead* and went to the record. What came back was more honest,
  in places more critical of the side the brief favoured, and built so it survives
  an opponent reading it with the sources open.

## What changed, and why it matters

The engine did three things the op-ed didn't:

1. **It refused the framing it was handed.** "Find the truth" beat "confirm my
   thesis." The cost-efficiency claim, the regional concentration, the live audit
   — all surfaced, even though they cut against the brief.
2. **It kept fact and interpretation visibly separate** — and still argued a clear
   public-interest view, openly, where the evidence allowed.
3. **It caught the claims that wouldn't hold.** Across the build, several
   confident, compelling claims failed verification (a flight booking, a minister's
   quote reversed on checking, named-tycoon loan write-offs, a privatisation "plan"
   that was really a "direction"). Each was downgraded or dropped. The pattern that
   survived rests on primary records, not slogans.

The lesson the whole system is built around: the honest version of an argument is
usually *available*, and usually *stronger* than the partisan one. The engine's
job is to find it.
